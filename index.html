Sound familiar?

Does this sound like you?

  • You're always a little anxious about money, even in a good month.
  • You don't really know where your salary goes, or how much you actually save.
  • You keep meaning to "sort out your finances", and another year goes by.

Nobody teaches us this at school or at work, so most of us figure it out alone, by trial and error. The good news is that money habits can be trained, just like fitness. That's where I come in.

The idea

Your financial health deserves the same care as your physical and mental health.

Money worries don't stay in your bank account. They follow you into your sleep, your work and your relationships. We wouldn't expect to get fit without a trainer, or get through a hard time without someone to talk to. Yet with money, most of us are on our own until something goes wrong.

    Why it happens

    What holds people back, and the habits we build instead.

    In conversations with people in their 20s, 30s and 40s, the same few patterns come up again and again. Most of them have little to do with income.

    What holds people backThe habit we build instead

    1. No idea where the money goes

      Without tracking, spending feels smaller than it is, and saving becomes whatever is left over.

      →

      Know your numbers

      A simple monthly picture of what comes in, what goes out and what stays.

    2. Lifestyle creep

      Every raise brings a bigger phone, car or holiday. Income rises, but savings stay where they were.

      →

      Save first, automatically

      Savings go out on payday, and rise every time your salary does.

    3. Waiting for the "right time"

      After the next bonus, after the wedding, once markets settle. Meanwhile, years of compounding slip by.

      →

      Start small, start now

      A routine you can keep beats a perfect plan you keep postponing.

    4. No safety net

      Without an emergency fund, every surprise becomes a credit card bill or a broken investment.

      →

      A cushion first

      Money set aside for surprises before investing, so they stop being emergencies.

    5. Investing on emotion and tips

      Buying what friends or social media are excited about, then selling in a panic when markets fall.

      →

      Goals before products

      Every rupee linked to a goal you care about, and reviewed on a schedule, not on headlines.

    6. No plan, and no one checking in

      Good intentions fade without clear goals and someone who holds you to them.

      →

      A trainer in your corner

      A check-in every quarter to catch slips early and keep you going.

    My story

    I started with ₹0 and a first salary of ₹14,200.

    That was 2014. I didn't come from money, and nobody around me talked about investing. Studying for the CFA charter taught me how money works. What actually moved the needle, though, was habit: saving first, staying invested through bad years, and not letting one decision undo the last ten.

    Twelve years of those habits later, I have saved and invested 33 times my family's annual expenses. That is financial independence, often called FIRE. I no longer need a salary to live the life we want.

    The biggest change wasn't the number. It was the peace of mind.

    I know how hard it is to build good habits alone. Now I want to be the trainer I wish I'd had.

    Questions

    Things people usually ask

    What does a financial fitness trainer actually do?

    The same thing a fitness trainer or nutritionist does, applied to money. We understand your current situation, diagnose the habits that help and hurt you, set goals together, and then meet regularly so you stay on track while those habits change.

    What happens in the free check-up?

    Three sessions. In the first, we look at where you are today. In the second, we diagnose the habits that help and hurt you. In the third, we agree the few changes that will make the biggest difference. There's no fee and no commitment, and you don't need to prepare anything.

    Will you try to sell me something?

    No. The free sessions are about you and your habits, not products. If you later want help investing in mutual funds, you can choose to do it through me, or anywhere else. There's no obligation either way.

    I'm embarrassed about my finances. Should I still reach out?

    Yes, please. Most people feel that way at first, and it usually means you're ready for a change. There's no judgement here, only a starting point.

    Can my spouse or partner join?

    Yes. Money is usually a family matter, and it helps when both of you are part of the conversation.

    Are you an investment adviser?

    No. I'm an AMFI-registered Mutual Fund Distributor, not a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser. The annual fee is for coaching and education on money habits, and doesn't include investment advice. If you choose to invest in mutual funds through me, I'm paid by commission from the fund houses, not by you.

    What happens after the three sessions?

    You decide. If you'd like to continue, you pick how to pay (a flat annual fee, or commission if you invest through me), and we meet every three months to review your progress. If not, you'll still leave with a much clearer picture of where you stand.

    What if I fall off track?

    You will, at some point. Everyone does. That is exactly what the quarterly check-ins are for: we notice it early, work out what got in the way, and adjust the plan so it fits your real life.

    Do I need a lot of money to start?

    No. Many people I talk to are just getting serious about saving. Starting early with a clear plan matters more than starting big.

    Can we meet online?

    Yes. We can meet in person in Mumbai, or on Google Meet from anywhere in India. You choose when you book.

    Can you promise returns?

    No one honestly can. Mutual fund returns depend on markets. What I can do is help you build good habits, stay disciplined, and avoid costly mistakes.
