Why it happens

What actually holds people back from their financial goals.

In conversations with people in their 20s, 30s and 40s, the same few patterns come up again and again. Most of them have little to do with income.

01

No idea where the money goes

Without tracking, spending feels smaller than it is and saving becomes whatever is left over, which is usually not much.

02

Lifestyle creep

Every raise brings a bigger phone, car or holiday. Income rises, but savings stay where they were.

03

Waiting for the "right time"

After the next bonus, after the wedding, once markets settle. Meanwhile, the most valuable years of compounding slip by.

04

No safety net

Without an emergency fund, every surprise becomes a credit card bill or a broken investment.

05

Investing on emotion and tips

Buying what friends or social media are excited about, then selling in a panic when markets fall.

06

No plan, and no one checking in

Good intentions fade without clear goals and someone who holds you to them.

Someone who invests ₹10,000 a month from age 30 to 55 could end up with about ₹1.24 crore. Starting at 35 instead, the same habit could grow to about ₹72 lakh. Same effort each month, five years later, roughly ₹52 lakh less.

None of these are fixed by a product. They are fixed by habits, and habits are easier to change with a trainer.

My story

I started with ₹0 and a first salary of ₹14,200.

That was 2014. I didn't come from money, and nobody around me talked about investing. Studying for the CFA charter taught me how money works. What actually moved the needle, though, was habit: saving first, staying invested through bad years, and not letting one decision undo the last ten.

Twelve years of those habits later, I have saved and invested 33 times my family's annual expenses. That is financial independence, often called FIRE. I no longer need a salary to live the life we want.

I know how hard it is to build good habits alone. Now I want to be the trainer I wish I'd had.

The diagnosis

Some of the habits we look at together.

Everyone has a mix. The point isn't to feel bad about the old ones. It's to swap them, one at a time, for ones that compound.

Habits that hold you back

  • –Saving whatever is left at the end of the month
  • –No emergency fund, so every surprise becomes debt
  • –Checking markets daily and reacting to the news
  • –Investing on tips from friends and social media
  • –Lifestyle growing every time income does

Habits we build instead

  • +Paying yourself first, automatically, every month
  • +A safety cushion set aside before investing
  • +Reviewing on a schedule, not on headlines
  • +Every rupee linked to a goal you care about
  • +Raising savings each time your salary rises
Staying on track

The plan is the easy part. Sticking to it is where a trainer helps.

Most people know roughly what they should do with money, just as most people know they should exercise. What changes things is someone who checks in, notices when you drift, and keeps you going when markets get noisy. Over time you build the financial muscle to make good decisions on your own.

  • A check-in every quarterYour goals, your habits and your progress, reviewed together.
  • Honest feedback when you slipSlips happen. We spot them early and get you back on track.
  • A call before big decisionsA home loan, a job change, a windfall. Talk it through before you commit.
  • We check in on how you feel, tooLower money anxiety is part of the goal, alongside the numbers.

Questions

Things people usually ask

What does a financial fitness trainer actually do?

The same thing a fitness trainer or nutritionist does, applied to money. We understand your current situation, diagnose the habits that help and hurt you, set goals together, and then meet regularly so you stay on track while those habits change.

Are the first three conversations really free?

Yes. There's no fee and no commitment. They're for understanding your situation and seeing whether we're a good fit.

What happens after the three conversations?

You decide. If you'd like to continue, you pick how to pay (a flat annual fee, or commission if you invest through me), and we meet every three months to review your progress. If not, you'll still leave with a much clearer picture of where you stand.

What if I fall off track?

You will, at some point. Everyone does. That is exactly what the quarterly check-ins are for: we notice it early, work out what got in the way, and adjust the plan so it fits your real life.

How do you earn money?

You choose. You can pay a flat annual fee for year-round coaching, or, if you invest in mutual funds through me, I earn commission from the fund houses and you pay no fee. There is no obligation to invest through me.

Do I need a lot of money to start?

No. Many people I talk to are just getting serious about saving. Starting early with a clear plan matters more than starting big.

Can we meet online?

Yes. We can meet in person in Mumbai, or on Google Meet from anywhere in India. You choose when you book.

Can you promise returns?

No one honestly can. Mutual fund returns depend on markets. What I can do is help you build good habits, stay disciplined, and avoid costly mistakes.
